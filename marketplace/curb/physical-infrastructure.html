Physical Infrastructure

The differentiating core of the Managed Marketplace model — managed intermediation isn't limited to digital.

It's embodied in four types of physical touchpoints:

Lockers
Self-service compartments, drop-off / pickup at any hour with no direct contact between parties. Design reference: Zurich's historical Milchkasten system.
Storefront corners
A display spot rented in an affiliated shop, for a high-volume depositor who wants to physically present items already listed online — a distributed proxy sale.
Warehouses
Peripheral storage, not meant to be visited by the public — a "warehouse" logic, not retail. Dedicated to storing bulky items collected from the street before they're listed.
Café tables
A bookable spot (table + chairs) in a café-type venue, for a handoff in a neutral, non-stigmatizing setting — with a possible side economy (on-site food and drink).

Common function of these four points: reduce the social friction of a direct encounter (awkwardness, mutual distrust) while re-inserting a commissionable node.

This is what makes an ad-free model economically viable.
